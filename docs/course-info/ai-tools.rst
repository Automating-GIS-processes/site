Use of AI tools
===============

Artificial intelligence (AI)-based tools using large language models (AI-LLMs), such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, or DeepL, are changing the ways in which people produce content including software.
Although these tools have great potential to expedite many coding tasks, it is important to note that one must understand the code that is produced in order to ensure it functions properly.
As the goal of the Automating GIS Processes course is to introduce python programming for GIS analysis, we (the teachers) must be careful to ensure the use of AI tools does not compromise the learning goals of the course.
Thus, we provide some guidelines for the use of AI tools below.

Guidelines for AI-LLM use in the Automating GIS processes course
----------------------------------------------------------------

.. note::

   Please note these guidelines apply only to students enrolled in the Automating GIS processes II course for credit.

1. Unless stated otherwise, AI-LLMs should not be used to generate code or answer questions in the course exercises.
2. Exercise problems or parts of problems where AI-LLM use is OK will be clearly indicated in the exercise assignment by the text "**AI-LLM OK**".
3. For problems or parts of problems where AI-LLM use is OK, students must indicate in writing which AI tool/model has been used and in what way.
4. If a student uses an AI-LLM for part of an exercise where it is prohibited or fails to report the use of an AI-LLM as instructed, this will be considered cheating and will be handled as such.

The guidelines above are based on the `University of Helsinki guidelines for the use of AI in teaching <https://teaching.helsinki.fi/instructions/article/artificial-intelligence-teaching>`_.
More information is available in the `instructions for students <https://studies.helsinki.fi/instructions/article/using-ai-support-learning>`_.

The *AI-assisted workflow* notebooks of the lessons demonstrate how to use AI assistants responsibly. The :doc:`final assignment <../final-assignment/final-assignment>` allows the use of AI-LLMs ("**AI-LLM OK**"), if you keep an AI-use log and verify the results.

.. admonition:: Students at the University of Helsinki
   :class: hint

   The University of Helsinki permits the use of generative AI in teaching and learning, unless a course or an assignment specifically restricts it. AI use should support your learning and follow the instructions given for each course: in this course, the guidelines above apply.

   If you use AI for your coursework, the University provides two services. They are the recommended options, particularly when your work involves course materials or other potentially sensitive information:

   - `CurreChat <https://curre.helsinki.fi/chat>`_: the University's own generative AI service, designed for educational use, with particular attention to data protection.
   - `Microsoft Copilot <https://m365.cloud.microsoft/chat>`_ with commercial data protection, available to students and staff.

   Read the `University of Helsinki guidelines on AI in teaching <https://teaching.helsinki.fi/instructions/article/artificial-intelligence-teaching>`_ for more information.



An introduction to AI-LLMs
--------------------------

In the Geo-Python course, we provide an introduction to using AI-LLMs for programming. It highlights the strengths and limitations of AI-generated code, shows how to test it, and explains why privacy matters when you use these tools. You can access it on the `Geo-Python course page <https://geo-python-site.readthedocs.io/en/latest/notebooks/L4/use-of-ai.html>`_.

.. note::

   Please note that we are adjusting to a changing environment as far as the AI-LLMs go, so we will do our best to encourage use where it makes sense.
   At the same time, we do not want to negatively impact your learning of core programming concepts.
